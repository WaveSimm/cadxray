To fit a PCB into an enclosure you need a 3D board with just the outline and the mounting holes. A DXF contains hundreds of things — silkscreen, dimensions, vias — so the heart of this exercise is **getting only what you need read**.

## Setup

1. Launch FreeCAD 1.1.3. You want `[CAD X-ray] 서버 시작` in the Report view.
2. Put both DXF files in `C:\cad\`.
3. In PowerShell, move to `C:\cad` and open `claude`.
4. Do **not** open the DXF in FreeCAD yourself (no File → Open). The reason is in step 1 below.

## Steps

### 1. Have it look at what is inside the DXF first

```text title="Say this"
C:\cad\MYB-6ULX_2D.dxf is a 2D PCB drawing. Don't open it in FreeCAD — read the file directly and show me a table of how many polylines and circles there are per layer name, whether the OUTLINE layer polyline is closed, and what circle radii exist.
```

**What the AI does** — reads the DXF text directly and extracts only the POLYLINE entries (layer, closed flag, vertices and bulge) and CIRCLE entries (layer, centre, radius) from the ENTITIES section. Use FreeCAD's DXF import and hundreds of silkscreen and dimension polylines each become a Draft object, which took over two minutes on MYB-6ULX when measured. That is why you say "don't open it in FreeCAD" from the start.

:::note[What the AI says back]
OUTLINE layer: 1 closed polyline, 8 vertices (bulge 0.4142 = 90° arcs at the four corners). Circles: four at radius 1.65 (Ø3.3), n others at 0.5 or more, and many below 0.5 (silkscreen dots and vias). The polylines on the other layers are silkscreen and dimensions.
:::

**How to check** — if the OUTLINE polyline is "closed" and the bulge values are near 0.4142, the board has corners rounded by 90° arcs. Four Ø3.3 circles are the mounting holes.

### 2. Tell it how to split the holes

```text title="Say this"
Use these rules.
- The outline is the OUTLINE layer polyline as-is. Segments with a bulge become arcs.
- Holes are only circles of radius 0.5 mm or more. Discard anything smaller (silkscreen dots and vias).
- Ø3.3 are mounting holes, so group them separately and put the diameter in Params.mount_d. Other holes keep their own diameters.
- If circles overlap on the same centre, keep one.
- Board thickness 1.6 mm, Params.thickness.
Show me the outline element count and the hole list (mounting / other) under those rules.
```

**What the AI does** — solves each bulge b into an angle θ = 4·atan(b), turning the span between two vertices into an arc, and filters circles by radius and centre into mounting holes and component pin holes.

**How to check** — you want 4 lines + 4 arcs = 8 outline elements and 4 mounting holes. The pin hole count varies by board.

### 3. Build the board

```text title="Say this"
Build the board in a new document pcb_MYB with the Body named MYB_6ULX.
- Pad: XY plane, the outline elements as-is, length Params.thickness
- PocketMount: the four mounting holes through, diameter Params.mount_d
- PocketPins: the remaining holes through
Set the Body's Label to MYB-6ULX. When you're done, tell me the sketch degrees of freedom, volume, bbox and face count.
```

**What the AI does** — transfers the outline lines and arcs into a sketch (with arc endpoints pinned to fixed points so the DoF reaches 0), then stacks one Pad and two Pockets in order, recomputing after each feature to check nothing broke.

:::note[What the AI says back]
MYB_6ULX done. PadBoard (8 elements, DoF 0) → PocketMount (4) → PocketPins (n). Shape valid, bbox about 70 × 55 × 1.6.
:::

**How to check** — in the tree, `PadBoard`, `PocketMount` and `PocketPins` should be in black text (no errors), and viewed from above (number key 2) the four corners should be rounded. The `Params` sheet holds `thickness`, `mount_d`, `width`, `height` and `corner_r`. The `width`, `height` and `corner_r` values are there for reference — the outline itself uses the DXF elements directly.

### 4. The second board, same rules

```text title="Say this"
Do MYS-6ULX_2D.dxf with the same rules, in a new document pcb_MYS with the Body MYS_6ULX.
```

**What the AI does** — applies the rules from the previous step unchanged. Once the rules are set, a board in the same family takes one line.

**How to check** — put the two documents side by side (Windows → Tile) and see how the outlines and mounting hole positions differ. Both are 6ULX family, so the sizes are similar.

### 5. Save

```text title="Say this"
Show me iso screenshots, and if they look right save them to C:\cad\pcb_MYB.FCStd and C:\cad\pcb_MYS.FCStd.
```

**How to check** — you should see the board thickness in the screenshot and the holes going all the way through.

## Checking your result

| Criterion | Value |
|---|---|
| Outline sketch | 8 elements (4 lines + 4 90° arcs), DoF 0 |
| Mounting holes | Ø3.3 × 4, `Params.mount_d` |
| Thickness | 1.6, `Params.thickness` |
| Shape validity | valid |
| Features | PadBoard → PocketMount → PocketPins |

## When it doesn't work

- **Nothing happens for over two minutes** — the AI used FreeCAD's DXF import after all. Wait for it to finish (it cannot be interrupted) and ask again with "don't open the DXF in FreeCAD, read the file directly".
- **The outline doesn't close and the Pad fails** — the OUTLINE polyline may be in several pieces, or under a different layer name (`Edge.Cuts`, `BOARD`). Pick the layer with a closed polyline from the table in step 1 and name it.
- **Too many holes** — raise the 0.5 radius threshold. Redefine it: "only radius 1.0 and above".
- **The mounting holes aren't Ø3.3** — boards vary (Ø3.2, Ø3.5). Look at the radii in the step 1 table and specify: "treat radius 1.6 as the mounting holes".
- **The arcs are inverted and the corners cut inward** — a bulge sign interpretation problem. Say "check the OUTLINE arcs bulge outward and fix the inverted ones".

## Going further

1. Build a "with components" outline Body by adding 15 mm of component height clearance above the board, and use it for enclosure interference checks (see the interference check in Exercise 4).
2. Change `Params.mount_d` to 3.5 for looser mounting holes.
3. Export both boards as STEP for your enclosure designer. "Export MYB_6ULX to C:\cad\MYB.step" is enough.
