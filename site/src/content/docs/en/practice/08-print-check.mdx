Finding out only after loading the slicer that your part needs a forest of supports, or that a wall will not print, sends you back to the design. This exercise checks and fixes the design inside FreeCAD before slicing. It does not slice.

## Setup

1. Launch FreeCAD 1.1.3 and confirm `[CAD X-ray] 서버 시작` in the Report view.
2. Open your AI tool.
3. Open a new document. Have your printer details ready: material (PLA, PETG, ABS, ASA, TPU, Nylon), nozzle diameter, layer height, bed size. If you do not know them, go with the defaults (PLA, 0.4, 0.2, 220×220×250).

## Steps

### 1. Build the test box

```text title="Say this"
New document, Body PrintBody as a test part:
- 40×30×20 box (floor at z=0)
- one rib on top, 0.6 thick, 5 tall
- a cantilever reaching 10 out from one side at z=10 (3 thick)
- another side face tilted to 70°
- one Ø3 vertical and one Ø6 horizontal through hole
Check it's valid and show me iso.
```

**What the AI does** — stacks Pads and Pockets into a Body, recomputes, checks validity and shows a screenshot. This shape is the same standard model (T12_print) cadxray used when verifying the checking feature.

**How to check** — `PrintBody` and its features are in the tree with no red marks.

### 2. State the profile and run the check

```text title="Say this"
Check PrintBody for printing. PETG, nozzle 0.6, layer 0.3, bed 220×220×250, no supports. Problems as a numbered list with type, severity, location and fix.
```

**What the AI does** — fills in the values the profile does not give (minimum wall thickness, overhang limit angle, maximum bridge length, hole compensation) from the material table of empirical values, and says it did so. It then converts the shape to a mesh and casts rays to check thin walls, the slope of downward-facing faces (overhangs, bridges, support area), small holes, horizontal holes, bed fit and first-layer contact area.

**How to check** — on this model, **three** should be caught: the thin wall, the cantilever overhang and the horizontal hole. The 70° face (inside the 45° limit), the Ø3 hole (above the 2.0 minimum) and the floor should not be.

:::note[What the AI says back]
Profile: PETG, nozzle 0.6, layer 0.3, bed 220×220×250. Minimum wall 1.2 (nozzle×2), overhang limit 45°, hole compensation 0.25 — all empirical values from the material table.
Score 85/100. Three problems:
1. Thin wall — rib 0.6 mm < minimum 1.2 (moderate). At (20, 15, 24). Fix: thicken
2. Overhang — 10 mm under the cantilever, support area about 200 mm² (moderate). Fix: a 45° wedge underneath (overhang_chamfer) or reorient
3. Horizontal hole — Ø6, will sag at the top (minor). Fix: teardrop
The 70° face is inside the 45° limit, so it is not an overhang, and Ø3 is above the 2.0 minimum hole size. The two vertical holes only appear as a shrinkage-compensation candidate (hole_comp +0.25). Bed fit: yes. Floor contact 975 mm².
:::

:::caution[The material table holds empirical values]
Overhang limit angles, hole compensation and shrinkage are at the level of slicer defaults and manufacturer guidance, not precise figures. Read the warnings the AI attaches as given, and do one test print on your own printer to calibrate.
:::

### 3. Pick a print orientation

```text title="Say this"
Orientation candidates, ranked.
```

**What the AI does** — scores the six directions (±X, ±Y, ±Z as the floor) together with the current orientation (six candidates when the current one is among them) by support area, contact area, height and bed fit, and ranks them. The top one is usually whichever eliminates the cantilever.

:::note[What the AI says back]
1. −Y as the floor: support 33 mm², contact 699, height 30, bed OK (score 101.7)
2. +Y as the floor: support 33, contact 699, height 30 (score 101.7)
3. Current (−Z): support 200, contact 975, height 25 (score 101.6)
…
Shall I apply number 1?
:::

```text title="Say this"
Apply number 1.
```

**How to check** — the part rotates in the 3D view and the floor lands at z=0. Say "check it again" and see that the cantilever overhang has gone. Since this exercise also practises design fixes, put it back to the original orientation once you have confirmed.

```text title="Say this"
Put it back to the original orientation.
```

### 4. Pick and apply design fixes

```text title="Say this"
Apply thicken the rib, wedge under the cantilever, hole compensation and teardrop. Leave the tilted face alone.
```

**What the AI does** — stacks each fix as a PartDesign feature. Thickening pads the thin face outward (re-measuring the thickness right before applying and skipping it if it is already sufficient); the wedge builds a helper Body filling to a 45° slope and fuses it; hole compensation is a Pocket enlarging the vertical hole by the compensation value; the teardrop is a 45° Pocket above the horizontal hole. Before applying, it tells you that «thickening grows outward and changes the outside dimensions» and «the wedge adds material and changes the weight».

**How to check** — new features (Chamfer, Pad, Pocket, Boolean) appear in the tree and the Body is valid. The volume before and after is in the answer. Any fix you dislike, delete its feature.

```text title="Say this"
Check again, remaining problems only.
```

**How to check** — thin walls should be 0 and the cantilever overhang should be gone. The teardrop's 45° faces sit right at the limit angle and may remain as a small overhang (about 84 mm²), and the horizontal hole keeps being reported even with the teardrop in place (the check does not recognise teardrops).

### 5. Split it if it exceeds the bed (optional)

```text title="Say this"
Pretend the bed is 30×30×30 and split PrintBody in two along x.
```

**What the AI does** — cuts along the axis and creates two pieces (each 30 or under) outside the Body, hiding the original. Joining pins and slots are manual.

### 6. Estimate weight, time and cost

```text title="Say this"
In PETG, what's the weight, filament length, time and cost? 3 walls, 20 % infill, 25,000 won per kg.
```

**What the AI does** — computes material volume (walls, infill, top and bottom surfaces) and reports weight (from the material density), 1.75 mm filament length, rough time and cost. It notes at the same time that this is coarser than a slicer, around ±30 %.

## Checking your result

| Item | Success criterion |
|---|---|
| First check | Thin wall 1 (0.6), the cantilever overhang, the Ø6 horizontal hole — three caught. The 70° face, the Ø3 hole and the floor are not caught |
| Orientation candidates | Seven, with the one that eliminates the cantilever first |
| After the fixes | Body valid, thin walls 0, cantilever support area down by more than half, vertical hole diameter grown by the compensation value, a teardrop above the horizontal hole |
| Splitting | Two pieces, each 30 or under, volumes summing to the original |
| Estimate | Weight = material volume × density |

## When it doesn't work

- **You didn't state a profile and it checked as PLA** — with nothing given, the AI uses PLA · 0.4 · 0.2 · 220×220×250 and says so. State the material and ask again.
- **The fix candidates come back as «manual» only** — curved thin walls and curved overhangs are not auto-fixable. Reorient, or change the design yourself.
- **A «not a Body» error** — automatic fixes only stack onto a PartDesign Body. Wrap a Part imported from STEP in a Body first, as in [Exercise 4](/cadxray/en/practice/04-step-clamp/).
- **Thickening was «skipped»** — when both faces of the same wall came back as candidates, applying one leaves the other already sufficient. That is expected.
- **You want to see the results in colour** — say "paint the faces with the results" and thin walls go red, supports orange, bridges yellow and floor contact green. "Restore the colours" puts it back.

## What runs behind the scenes

| Step | Tool |
|---|---|
| Checking, scoring, candidates | `check_printability` (`paint` to colour it) |
| Orientation candidates and applying | `suggest_orientation` |
| The six design fixes | `apply_print_fixes` (elephant_foot · hole_comp · teardrop · thicken · overhang_chamfer · split) |
| Weight, time, cost | `estimate_print` |
